About Danvers Pro Plumbing

We run a plumbing dispatch line for Danvers and four nearby towns. This page explains plainly what that means and what to expect.

What We Are

Danvers Pro Plumbing is a dispatch-based service. You call (855) 575-2890, a dispatcher listens, and a licensed and insured plumber is sent to your property. The business address is 99 Conifer Hill Dr, Danvers, MA 01923.

We do not publish owner names, team sizes or job counts on this site. What we can describe is how a call works, what the plumber must hold and how the price is handled.

How The Line Works

Calling (855) 575-2890 reaches dispatch at any hour. The line answers on nights, weekends and holidays, because a burst pipe or a sewer backup does not wait for business hours. The dispatcher asks for your street address, your town and what is happening, then sends a licensed plumber to the property.

The questions on the call are practical ones. Is water still running, and can you reach the main shutoff? Is the problem one fixture or the whole house? Are there signs of gas, which would change everything? The answers help the plumber arrive with the right tools and help us tell a true emergency from a repair that can wait for a daytime slot.

This site is call-only. There are no contact forms to fill out and no quote requests to wait on, because a plumbing problem is usually faster to explain by voice than by typing.

What Licensed And Insured Means

In Massachusetts, only a master or journeyman plumber licensed by the Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters may do plumbing work. The state lets anyone check a license through its eLIPSE lookup on mass.gov, under Check an Occupational Board License. We encourage you to look up the plumber who arrives.

Insured means the plumber carries liability coverage for accidental damage during the job. Ask for proof if you want it. We do not post license numbers or insurer names on this site, because the right check is the state lookup, not a webpage.

How A Visit Runs

The plumber starts by listening to what you saw and heard, then looks at the fixture, the pipe or the appliance in question. Where a drain or sewer line is involved, a camera or a cable machine may be used to find the cause before anyone proposes a fix. Where a leak is involved, the first job is to stop the water.

Next comes a plain explanation. You hear what failed, why it likely failed and what the options are, including the cheaper one when a cheaper one is sensible. Some jobs are a repair and some are a replacement, and an older water heater or a worn-out fixture can be the second kind even when you hoped for the first.

After you agree, the work is done, the area is tidied and the plumber tests the repair before leaving. If the job needs a permit, the licensed plumber handles that step, since permits go only to licensed plumbers. Our services page lists the kinds of jobs we dispatch for, from drain cleaning to water heater replacement.

Clear Price Before Work Starts

The plumber explains the likely cause and the proposed fix, then states a clear price before work starts. Nothing begins until you agree. Final costs depend on access, parts and the condition of the existing pipe, which is why we do not list prices online.

What Shapes The Price

We keep dollar figures off this site on purpose. Two houses with the same symptom can have very different jobs. A clogged line may be a short cable run in one home and a root-filled section of old pipe in another. Access, parts, the condition of the existing pipes and the hour of the call all matter.

What you can count on is the order of events. The plumber diagnoses first, states a clear price second and starts work only after you say yes. If the number does not suit you, you can decline, and a safe temporary fix is still worth discussing when water or gas is involved.

Permits And Inspections

State rules require a permit from the local plumbing inspector before plumbing is installed, altered or replaced. Permits go only to licensed plumbers. In Danvers, Inspectional Services at 1 Sylvan Street handles local permits. Minor repairs such as fixing a leaky faucet or clearing a blocked drain need no permit.

What We Do Not Do

We are not the Town of Danvers. The Town Water Division supplies the water and the Town Sewer Division maintains the main line sewer in the street. We cannot repair the Town's main, and we will say so when a camera shows that a problem sits there. The Town also owns the water service up to the curb stop, so a leak on that side is a call to the Town.

We do not handle septic tanks. In towns such as Middleton, many homes use septic systems, and a plumber works on the house plumbing, not the tank. If a backup looks like a septic question, we will tell you to bring in a septic professional first.

We do not hand out license numbers or insurer names on this site, and we do not invent reviews, awards or job counts. We also do not send unlicensed workers to do plumbing work that the state reserves for licensed plumbers. If a gas smell or a carbon monoxide alarm is the reason for your call, leave the house and call 911 before you call us.
