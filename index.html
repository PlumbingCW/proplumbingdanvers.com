Danvers Water, Sewer And Housing: What They Mean For Your Pipes

Danvers runs its own Water Division, which also supplies Middleton. Most of the supply starts at Middleton Pond and is treated at a plant on Lake Street, so you are not on MWRA water.

According to Massachusetts standards, only a master or journeyman plumber licensed by the state may do plumbing work, and anyone can check a license through the eLIPSE lookup on mass.gov.

The town's report notes that white scale on faucets and shower heads can come from calcium carbonate, so if you see crust on fixtures, ask the Water Division for its annual water quality report before blaming the fixture.

Older houses, older pipes

About 25 percent of Danvers homes were built in 1939 or earlier, and roughly 70 percent predate 1980. Homes that old can still carry galvanized supply lines or aging drain pipe, so a repeat leak often points to the pipe, not the fitting.

The town owns the main sewer

The Town Sewer Division maintains the gravity sewer main in the street. If a backup affects several neighbors, the main may be involved, so a camera look at your own line is the quickest way to learn whose problem it is.

Curb stop divides the water line

The town owns and repairs the water service up to and including the curb stop. From there to your house, the pipe is yours, so a leak on that stretch is a plumbing job, not a call to the town.

Sump pumps stay off the sewer

Danvers prohibits connecting a sump pump to the town sewer, with no grandfathering. If your pump discharges to a floor drain, expect a plumber to reroute it outdoors.

Villages, Flood Zones And Where We Dispatch

Danvers is more than one neighborhood. Danvers Center, Ferncroft, Burley's Corner, Tapleyville, Putnamville and Danversport each have their own street patterns, and Hathorne has its own post office and ZIP code 01937. Give dispatch your street address and the village name, since the mailing town on a bill does not always match what neighbors call the area.

Water history matters here too. The town's hazard planning lists flooding near Town Center, the Porter River and Beaver Brook, and rates flood risk as high. Danversport sits at the tidal Danvers River, with a marina and John George Park on that shoreline.

If your basement has taken on water or your floor drain has bubbled during a storm, ask a plumber about a backwater valve. The town's sewer page, citing the state plumbing code, requires one where basement sewage discharges below the rim of the nearest public sewer manhole.

The Salem Village Historic District falls inside Danvers as well, and houses in historic districts often have old piping worth inspecting before a renovation.

The First Minutes: What To Do Before The Plumber Arrives

Do These While You Wait

Water damage grows by the minute, so a few quick steps protect your floors and belongings.

  • 1Find the main water shut-off, usually near the meter or where the line enters the basement, and close it.
  • 2Switch off the breaker to any room with standing water or a wet outlet.
  • 3Move rugs, boxes and electronics out of the wet zone.
  • 4Turn off the water heater if the tank is leaking or the burner is running dry.
  • 5Photograph the damage for your insurance claim before you clean up.

If you smell gas, skip this list. Leave the building and call 911 or the gas utility from outside.

Licensed Plumbers And Permits In Massachusetts

Only a master or journeyman plumber licensed by the Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters may do plumbing work in Massachusetts, and anyone can check a license through the state eLIPSE lookup on mass.gov. State rules also require a permit from the local plumbing inspector before plumbing is installed, altered or replaced, and permits go only to licensed plumbers.

Minor repairs such as fixing a leaky faucet or clearing a blocked drain need no permit, but replacing or relocating a faucet does. Danvers Inspectional Services at 1 Sylvan Street handles permits locally.
