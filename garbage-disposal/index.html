Humming, Stuck, Silent or Dripping

A garbage disposal grinds food with a spinning plate and two small lugs, driven by a motor under the sink. The grinding chamber is bolted to the drain at the sink flange, and a discharge pipe carries waste to the trap. The symptom tells you which of those has failed.

Under the state rule in 248 CMR 3.05, no plumbing work may be installed, altered, removed, replaced or repaired until a permit is issued, and that standard covers a disposal swap too.

Hums but does not spin

The plate is jammed, often by a bone, fruit pit or utensil. The motor is working against the jam and can burn out if left running.

Completely silent

The overload protector has tripped, or the outlet or wall switch has lost power. The red reset button may fix it.

Water under the sink

The leak comes from the sink flange, the dishwasher hose, the discharge pipe or a cracked housing. A dry paper towel test shows which.

Sink fills while it runs

The drain line is blocked beyond the disposal, so ground waste cannot leave. It is a plumbing clog, not a motor failure.

Jam Safety Steps

Before you touch anything in the drain:

  • 1Turn the wall switch off and unplug the unit, or switch off its breaker.
  • 2Shine a flashlight inside and pull out any object with tongs or pliers, never your fingers.
  • 3Insert a hex key into the socket at the bottom of the unit and turn it back and forth to free the plate.
  • 4Press the red reset button on the underside, restore power and run cold water as you test it.

If it trips again immediately, stop and call. Repeated resets can overheat the motor.

What Never Belongs Down the Disposal

  • Grease, fat and cooking oil, which coat the discharge pipe and harden in the trap
  • Coffee grounds in quantity, which pack into a paste
  • Potato peels and pasta, which swell into a gluey mass
  • Celery, corn husks and other fibers that wrap the plate
  • Bones, fruit pits and shells, which jam the lugs
  • Chemical drain cleaners, which attack seals and splash when you reach in

Run a strong flow of cold water for about fifteen seconds after each use so the pieces reach the main drain. Hot water melts grease briefly, then lets it set further down the line.

When the Drain Line Is the Real Problem

A disposal sends solids into a small kitchen branch line, and that is where the damage builds. If the sink backs up even when the unit is working, the pipe past the trap is partly blocked with grease. A cable pass fixes it, and a stubborn buildup may call for hydro jetting to strip the pipe wall.

Danvers asks residents not to flush so-called flushable wipes because they do not break down, and the same logic applies to a kitchen line. In houses from before 1960, which is about 43.5 percent of Danvers housing, the kitchen drain may be old cast iron or galvanized steel with a rough interior that catches debris. Replacing a Garbage Disposal

Replace the unit if the housing leaks, the motor will not restart after a clean reset, or it jams over and over. Horsepower matters: a quarter or a third horsepower model struggles with heavy use, while a half horsepower and up grinds more finely. Continuous-feed units run while the switch is on, and batch-feed units need a stopper in place.

Most swaps reuse the mounting ring and drain connections, but the electrical connection needs care. A hardwired unit should be disconnected at the breaker first. State rules say no plumbing is installed or replaced until a permit is issued and that permits go only to licensed plumbers, so ask us how the swap is handled.
