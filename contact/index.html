Why We Take Calls, Not Forms

Plumbing problems change by the minute, so a form that waits in an inbox does not help. A phone call lets a dispatcher ask what is leaking and what you have already tried. This site has no contact forms, no live chat and no booking widget.

What To Have Ready When You Call

Four Things Dispatch Will Ask

You do not need technical words. A few facts speed up the call.

  • 1The full address, including the town and a cross street if you know it.
  • 2What is happening: where the water is, what it looks like and how fast it is coming.
  • 3Whether you have found the main shut-off and closed it.
  • 4A callback number in case the line drops.

If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 or the gas utility first.

After You Call

The dispatcher explains the next step, which is usually sending a licensed and insured plumber. You get a clear price before work starts. If a visit can wait until morning, ask about same-day appointments, which are offered when available.
