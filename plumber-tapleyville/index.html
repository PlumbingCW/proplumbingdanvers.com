A Village Built Around Carpet Mills

The town's open space plan says Tapleyville emerged in the 1830s as a center for woven carpets. The state historical survey of Danvers adds that the village was settled before 1754 and ties it to Gilbert Tapley, who established a woolen carpet mill in 1843. The survey records early industrial brick houses there, along with a mansard-roofed Tapley house and the 1896 Tapley School.

History does not tell you what is inside your walls. Houses from several eras can sit on the same street in an old village, and the only way to know what pipes you have is to look at them. A plumber can open an access panel or run a camera down the line, and that is cheaper than guessing.

What Danvers Housing Age Means for Drains and Supply Lines

The Census Bureau's 2020 to 2024 estimates show that about 25.4 percent of Danvers homes were built in 1939 or earlier, about 43.5 percent before 1960 and about 69.5 percent before 1980. Those are town-wide numbers. We do not claim that any Tapleyville street has cast iron, galvanized or clay pipe, because no source says so.

What an older house does mean is more years of wear on fixtures, shutoff valves and water heaters. That is a reason to ask for an inspection before something fails on a holiday weekend. A short visit can tell you which parts are fine and which are worth planning for.

Slow Drains, Repeat Clogs and Sewer Lines

One slow sink is usually a local clog, and drain cleaning clears it. If several fixtures slow down together, or the same clog returns within weeks, the problem is probably further along the line. A sewer camera inspection shows whether roots, grease, a belly or a break is behind it.

According to the Town of Danvers, so-called flushable wipes do not break down, and they clog sewer pipes. Putting them in the trash is the cheapest drain care there is. The town maintains the sewer main under the street, so if neighbors have the same backup, call the Danvers Sewer Division too.

Water Heaters, Scale and Hot Water Problems

The town's water report notes that white scale on faucets and shower heads may come from calcium carbonate. It publishes no hardness figure, so we do not give one. Ask the Danvers Water Division for its annual water quality report. Scale can also collect inside a tank water heater, which is one reason an aging tank is worth checking.

Massachusetts plumbing code requires a properly sized temperature and pressure relief valve within 12 inches of the top of a vertical storage heater (248 CMR 10.14). Its discharge pipe must be full size, have no shutoff valve and end at a fixture or within 12 inches of the floor. If yours is capped, missing or running into a bucket, say so when you call.

Replacing a water heater counts as replacing plumbing under the state's permit rule, so the local inspector must issue a permit before the work starts and only a licensed plumber can pull it. Ask Danvers Inspectional Services about your specific job. Our water heater replacement page lists what to expect.

Leaks You Cannot See

A higher water bill with no change in habits, a damp spot that returns or the sound of running water when everything is off all point to a leak. The town owns the water service up to and including the curb stop, and the owner is generally responsible from there to the building. Leak detection pinpoints the pipe so only the damaged section gets opened.

If detection shows pipes that have leaked more than once, repiping can replace the weak runs instead of patching them one at a time. A plumber can show you the damaged sections so you can judge for yourself.
