Emergency Water Service Line Repair in Danvers, MA

A service line leak can hide for weeks, then show up as a soggy lawn or a pressure drop. When the pipe from the street to your house fails, you need water service line repair in Danvers, MA, and our 24/7 emergency line is open for it. Close the main valve inside the house while you wait.

The service line is the single pipe that carries town water from the main under the street, through the curb stop and the meter, into your home. Ownership of that pipe is split, and knowing where the split falls saves a lot of phone calls.

Who Owns Which Part of the Line

The Town of Danvers owns, repairs and maintains the water service up to and including the curb stop. The owner generally takes over from the curb stop to the building when the stop sits in the public way, and owns the service pipe on private property. So a leak at the street connection is a call to the town, while a leak under your driveway or lawn is yours.

That boundary decides who pays, which is why the first thing a plumber does is locate the curb stop and trace the pipe toward the house. If the town's side is the problem, we tell you so rather than dig on your property. If you think the leak is on the street side, contact the Town of Danvers DPW Water Division.

Dig, Slide or Pull: Trench Versus Trenchless

Replacing a buried service pipe can be done in two broad ways. The right one depends on what sits above the line and how long it is.

Choosing a repair method for a buried water service
MethodBest whenDisruptionTrade-off
Open trenchShort run, open lawn, a clear leak locationDug channel from the break to sound pipeStraightforward and visible, but lawn, landscaping or a driveway edge needs restoring
Spot repairOne failed fitting or short crack, pipe otherwise goodA small hole at the leakQuick and low cost, but old pipe may fail again nearby

Full replacementCorroded old line with repeated leaksTrench or trenchless over the whole runHighest upfront work, but ends the repair cycle

Trenchless methods avoid cutting a long trench, but they are not magic. A line with sharp bends, other buried utilities close by or a collapsed section may still need an open excavation. We explain the method on site and show you the reason for it.

Soggy Lawn or Spinning Meter: Signs of a Leaking Service

Wet ground, no rain

A soft or sunken strip of lawn between the curb and the house, especially in dry weather, often sits right on top of the pipe.

Meter still moving

With every fixture and appliance off, a meter dial that keeps turning means water is leaving the system somewhere between the meter and the taps.

Water bills that jump

A bill noticeably higher than your usual with no change in habits is a reason to check the meter before assuming the worst.

Water in the basement at one wall

Dampness where the line enters the foundation can come from a service pipe leak, not only groundwater. A plumber can tell the two apart.

If you cannot tell whether the loss is inside the house or outside it, our leak detection service listens to the pipe and pinpoints the break so only the necessary ground is opened.

Weak Pressure at Every Faucet

Low pressure that affects the whole house can come from several places, and a service line problem is only one of them. A half-closed main, a failed pressure reducer, a clogged strainer or a corroded pipe all choke flow. Galvanized lines in older houses are a common cause (see repiping for the inside-the-house side), because rust builds up inside until the opening is a fraction of its original size.

Before anyone digs, we check the simple items first: the main valve position, a pressure gauge on an outdoor spigot, and whether one floor is worse than another. A reading taken at the hose bib tells us if the problem starts at the street or inside the walls.

A Careful Word on Old Lead Lines

Some older homes across the region once had lead or lead-joined service pipes. In 2023 sampling, the Danvers 90th percentile for lead at the tap was 4.7 ppb, with one of 32 sites above 15 ppb, so results at the tap are generally low. Still, the pipe material on your own property is something you can check, and a plumber can identify it by scratching a short exposed section.

If your house is old and the line has never been replaced, ask the Danvers Water Division what it knows about your service. When we open a trench for any reason, we will tell you what the pipe is made of, and replacing it at that moment costs less than a second dig later.

Meter, Curb Stop and the Valves in Between

Between the street and your house the water passes through the curb stop, a shutoff the town uses to cut service, then the meter, then your main valve. A stuck or leaking main valve is a common reason a house cannot be isolated, and we often replace it during a service repair so the next emergency is a one-turn fix.

A wet yard can also come from a cracked drain, which our sewer line repair page covers. The meter itself belongs to the water system and is not something we alter. If you suspect the meter is misreading, call the Water Division. If the meter reads correctly and water is truly leaking, the pipe is the cause.

What Drives The Price Of Service Line Work

Staying Safe Until We Arrive

  • Close the main valve inside the house.
  • Keep children and pets off soft or sinking ground over the pipe line.
  • Do not run the dishwasher or washing machine, since a partly filled system can drain back through the leak.
  • Note the meter reading and the time so we can confirm the loss.
  • Photograph the wet area before it dries.
