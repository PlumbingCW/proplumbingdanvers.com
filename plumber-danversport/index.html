Where Danversport Sits and Why It Matters for Plumbing

Danversport grew from the mills that Archelaus Putnam set up on the Crane River in 1754, and it was once called New Mills. It later became a shipbuilding and shipping center along the Porter River, according to the state historical survey of Danvers. Both rivers matter to a homeowner today, because the town's own resilience report lists the Porter River and the Crane River among the places in Danvers that experience flooding.

The town's coastal vulnerability work names Danversport, including residential and commercial properties, a marina and John George Park, and says the harbor needs protection from sea level rise and storm surge. That is a town planning statement, not a forecast for your street. The practical takeaway is simple. If your basement, floor drain or sump pit is the lowest point in a low-lying area, check it before the storm and not during it.

Backwater Valves, Floor Drains and the Town Sewer

The town's Sewer Division maintains more than 125 miles of gravity sewer main, and the flow ends up at the South Essex Sewerage District plant in Salem. If a blockage sits in the main under the street, the town deals with it. If it sits in the pipe that runs from your house to the street, a plumber does. When several neighbors back up at once, call the Danvers Sewer Division as well as your plumber.

Danvers requires a backwater valve where a basement's sewage discharge sits below the rim of the closest public sewer manhole, and the valve goes in before the sewer connection leaves the building. The town's page cites 248 CMR 10.00. A web page cannot tell you whether your house meets that test. A plumber can compare your lowest fixtures with the manhole rim and tell you. The town's resilience workshop also listed sewer backflow prevention among its recommended actions.

Sump pumps have a rule of their own. Danvers does not allow a sump pump to connect to the town sewer, and there is no grandfathering. Roof downspouts, exterior foundation drains and area drains may not connect to the building sewer either. If your pump's discharge line ends at a basement drain, mention it when you call, because it needs to be corrected. Our sump pump service page explains testing, repair and replacement.

What To Do While Water Is Rising or a Drain Is Backing Up

Stop adding water

Pause the washer, the dishwasher and any showers. Everything you run goes into the same blocked line.

Count the affected drains

If the basement floor drain and the lowest tub back up together, the problem is likely past your branch lines. One slow sink is usually a clog at that sink.

Keep power away from water

Stay out of standing water near outlets, the panel and a sump pump cord. If water has reached them, call an electrician or the electric utility.

Photograph the damage

Take photos of the water line and any wet belongings before you start cleaning. They help if you file an insurance claim later.

Water Service, Curb Stops and Main Breaks

On September 23 and 24, 2025, Danvers Public Works crews repaired a water main break first reported on South Liberty Street that interrupted service in the Water Street area, according to Patch. If your water goes out and a neighbor's does too, call the town before you assume your own pipe has failed.

The town owns, repairs and maintains the water service up to and including the curb stop. The owner is generally responsible from the curb stop to the building when the stop is in the public way, and for the service pipe on the owner's property. A leak between the curb box and your house is therefore usually yours to fix. A leak detection visit finds where it is before anyone digs.

Older Homes and What the Numbers Do and Do Not Say

Across Danvers, the Census Bureau's 2020 to 2024 estimates show that about 25.4 percent of homes were built in 1939 or earlier and about 69.5 percent before 1980. Those are town-wide figures, not Danversport figures, so we do not assume what is inside your walls. A sewer camera inspection shows the real condition of a drain line, and it costs far less than a surprise failure.
