Whole House Repiping in Danvers, MA

Whole house repiping in Danvers, MA means replacing the water supply pipes inside your walls, floors and crawl space when they have reached the end of their life. It is a big job, so the first question is whether you need one at all or whether targeted repairs will do.

This page explains what ages pipe, what the warning signs look like, how PEX and copper compare, and what a repipe involves for walls and permits.

Why Danvers Houses Reach the Repipe Question

Census data puts 25.4 percent of Danvers housing, about 2,930 homes, at 1939 or earlier, and about 69.5 percent before 1980. Pipe from those eras was often galvanized steel or early copper, and some houses have had a mix of both added over the decades.

That mix matters. Galvanized pipe rusts from the inside, narrowing the bore until pressure drops. Copper lasts longer but can pit, and where copper meets steel without a dielectric fitting, corrosion speeds up. A house with a 1950s kitchen and a 1990s bath may therefore have three different pipe ages in one wall.

Signs Your Pipes Are Failing

Brown or orange water

Rust is flaking from galvanized pipe walls. If it clears after running a few minutes, the rust is coming from the pipe and not the main.

Weak flow at several fixtures

Mineral and rust buildup closes the bore. Pressure at the street can be fine while the faucet barely trickles.

Repeated pinhole leaks

Green stains or tiny sprays on copper mean the wall is thin. Fix one and another appears nearby.

Ceiling or wall stains

Water finding its way through framing can run a long way from the source. See leak detection to trace it before opening walls.

Repipe or Spot Repair: How We Decide

A single failed joint or one section above the water heater is a spot repair, and we say so. The picture changes when leaks recur, when pressure falls house-wide or when the pipe material is known to fail, as with polybutylene.

A rough rule: if you have fixed three leaks in a year, or if you can see pitting at every joint we open, the pipe is telling you it is done. Patching an old line that is failing everywhere simply moves the leak down the line. Your own plans count too. Selling in a year, or opening a bathroom wall for a remodel, may tip the choice.

Questions That Settle It

  • ✓Is the pipe material one of the known failure types?
  • ✓How many leaks in the past two years?
  • ✓Is low pressure house-wide or one fixture?
  • ✓Are walls already open for a remodel?
  • ✓Do you plan to stay or sell soon?

Two or three yes answers usually point toward repiping.

Copper is the choice when pipes sit exposed in a finished basement or when you want a traditional material. PEX wins on speed and on fewer openings in plaster or tile. Either can be right. Danvers sits above a frost line of 48 inches, so any run that crosses an unheated crawl space or exterior wall needs insulation whichever pipe you choose.

What Happens to Your Walls

Flexible PEX can be fished through existing cavities from the basement and attic, so most access holes are small and sit in closets, behind fixtures or at the ends of runs. Copper needs more openings. Plaster and lath, common in houses from before 1940, are slower to open and patch than drywall.

We plan runs to minimize cuts and protect floors. Patch and paint is normally a separate trade, and we say where that line falls before starting. Most homes keep water for at least part of each day, since the job proceeds room by room.

Safety Checklist Before We Arrive

  • Locate your main water shutoff and make sure it turns.
  • Clear the basement and the area around the water heater so the crew can reach the pipes.
  • Move furniture and valuables away from walls where pipes will be opened.
  • Plan a period without running water on the day of the work, and fill a few containers beforehand.
  • Photograph any stains or wet spots so we can see where leaks started.

Permits and Licensing

Massachusetts requires the local plumbing inspector to issue a permit before plumbing is installed, and under 248 CMR 3.05 no plumbing work may be installed, altered, removed, replaced or repaired until one is issued. Permits go only to licensed plumbers, and only a master or journeyman plumber licensed by the state may do the work.

Danvers Inspectional Services at 1 Sylvan Street handles local permits. You can verify any plumber through the state license lookup on mass.gov. A repipe done without a permit can complicate a sale or an insurance claim later.
