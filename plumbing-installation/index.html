Rough-In First, Finish Second

Every installation splits into two phases. Rough-in is the plumbing that goes inside walls and floors: drain and vent pipes, supply lines, stub-outs and shutoff valves. Finish work comes after drywall and tile, when the fixtures, faucets and trim go on.

New plumbing in Massachusetts is governed by the Uniform State Plumbing Code, 248 CMR 10.00, which is the standard a licensed plumber installs to.

The two phases are separated by inspection. The local inspector looks at the roughed-in pipe before it is covered, which is why a plumber is booked twice on a bathroom project. Cover the walls too early and the inspector can ask you to open them again.

Remodels in Older Danvers Houses

About 43.5 percent of Danvers homes were built before 1960, and about 69.5 percent before 1980. Opening a wall in one of those houses often reveals galvanized supply, cast iron drains or older vents that do not meet current layout. Allow for this early, since adding a bathroom may mean replacing a stack that was never sized for it.

That is the practical reason to hire the plumber before the contractor closes the budget. If the old pipe is failing anyway, repiping the affected branch while the wall is open costs less than doing it later.

New Construction Plumbing

For a new house or an addition, plumbing starts with the site: where the water service enters, where the sewer connection exits and how deep the lines must sit. Massachusetts frost line depth is 48 inches, so a buried water line has to go below that to stay out of freezing ground.

The Town of Danvers requires a backwater valve where basement sewage discharge is below the rim elevation of the closest public sewer manhole. The valve goes in before the sewer connection exits the building. The town also bars sump pumps, roof downspouts, foundation drains and area drains from the building sewer. Plan the sump discharge to the yard. Our sump pump page covers it.

Water Softeners and Filters

Before you buy any treatment equipment, find out what is in your water. The Danvers water report notes that white scale on faucets and shower heads may be calcium carbonate, but the town does not publish a hardness figure. Ask the Water Division for its annual water quality report, then test your tap.

A cheap home test strip gives a rough hardness reading, and a lab test is more exact. If the water is hard, a salt-based softener helps scale in kettles, fixtures and water heaters. A carbon filter at the kitchen sink improves taste, and under-sink reverse osmosis cleans drinking water further.

Permits and Licensed Work

The Commonwealth requires the local plumbing inspector to issue a permit before plumbing is installed, and permits go only to licensed plumbers. Under 248 CMR 3.05 no plumbing may be installed, altered, removed, replaced or repaired until a permit is issued. Replacing or relocating a faucet counts too, though a leaky faucet fix does not.

Danvers Inspectional Services at 1 Sylvan Street handles local permits. No Danvers page states when a permit is required for a given job, so ask there if you are unsure. Only a master or journeyman plumber licensed by the state may do plumbing work, and you can verify a license on mass.gov.
