Water Is Spraying Right Now: The First Ten Minutes

Do these while you wait for the plumber

Speed matters more than neatness. A running pipe can drop hundreds of gallons into a finished basement in an hour or so.

  • ✓Close the main shutoff valve, usually a lever or round wheel where the water line enters the house near the meter.
  • ✓Open the lowest cold faucet in the house to drain what is left in the pipes and relieve pressure.
  • ✓Switch off the breaker for any room with water near outlets, but only if you can reach the panel without standing in water.
  • ✓Move rugs, boxes and electronics out of the wet area, and put a bucket or towels under active drips.
  • ✓Take photos of the damage for your insurance claim before cleanup starts.

Inside the house, the danger is different: a pipe in an exterior wall, an unheated crawl space or a garage ceiling can freeze at much shallower exposure, swell and split at the weak spot, then leak only when it thaws.

Reading the Break: Signs a Pipe Has Cracked

Water stains that spread

A brown ring on a ceiling that grows after you use water upstairs points to a supply or drain leak above it. Stains that appear overnight are usually a fresh break.

Sudden pressure loss

Faucets that sputter or run weak across the whole house suggest a main break or a large leak on the supply side. One weak fixture is more likely a local issue.

Sound in the walls

Hissing or a steady trickle when every fixture is off means water is escaping under pressure. Banging that begins after a cold snap can mean ice is moving in a line.

A water meter that will not sit still

Shut every fixture off and watch the small dial or flow triangle on the meter. If it keeps turning, water is going somewhere it should not.

Frozen Pipes: Thawing Safely and Preventing the Split

If a faucet gives you nothing in January and the room is cold, a pipe is probably frozen. Open that faucet, then warm the pipe slowly with a hair dryer or by raising the room temperature. Never use a torch or open flame, which can ignite framing and crack the pipe.

Once a frozen pipe thaws, watch it closely for the next hour. If water drips or stains appear, close the main and call. The 48 inch frost depth fact applies to buried lines, so a street-side water service freeze is a different problem from an attic line freeze and often needs a plumber with digging equipment.

  • Keep cabinet doors open on exterior walls during a hard freeze so room air reaches the pipes.
  • Let a faucet drip on the coldest nights if that line has frozen before.
  • Disconnect garden hoses and shut off outdoor spigots from inside in the fall.
  • Insulate pipes in garages, crawl spaces and above basement windows.
