Water On The Floor Around The Tank

Where the water appears matters more than how much. Drips at the top usually come from a loose supply fitting or the relief valve. Water seeping from the bottom of the jacket, or a slow dampness at the edge of the pan, often means the steel tank has rusted through, which no repair can fix.

For a leak, shut the cold water valve at the top of the heater, cut power at the breaker or set the gas control to pilot or off, and run a hose from the drain valve if you can. Do not touch a tank that is hot to the point of steaming. We come out around the clock for leaks, and a firm price comes before any part is ordered.

Leaking water heater: first five minutes

Do these in order if it is safe to reach the tank.

  • 3Move stored items away from the base

If you smell gas, leave the house and call the gas utility from outside.

Why The Hot Water Stopped

No hot water has a short list of causes, and the cause depends on fuel type. A gas heater with a pilot that will not stay lit usually has a worn thermocouple or a dirty pilot tube. An electric heater that runs lukewarm often has one dead element, while a tank that gives no heat at all may have a tripped high-limit switch or a failed upper thermostat.

Pilot, Thermostat, Element And Anode Problems

The pilot assembly is the most common gas failure. The thermocouple sits in the flame and tells the gas valve that it is safe to stay open, so when it weakens, the valve shuts and the flame dies. Replacing it is a small job when the control valve is healthy.

According to 248 CMR 10.14, a storage heater needs a properly sized temperature and pressure relief valve within 12 inches of the top of a vertical tank, and a repair visit checks that standard.

Electric heaters have two thermostats and two elements, and each can fail on its own. We test with a meter instead of swapping parts at random. The anode rod is a magnesium or aluminum bar inside the tank that corrodes first, so the steel does not. When it is consumed, the tank starts to rust, so replacing a spent anode can add years if done before the tank is pitted.

The Relief Valve On Top Is Not A Decoration

Massachusetts plumbing code, 248 CMR 10.14, calls for a properly sized temperature and pressure relief valve within 12 inches of the top of a vertical tank. The discharge pipe must be full size, with no shut-off valve and no cap, and it must end at a fixture or within 12 inches of the floor. A valve that drips often or discharges steadily signals either high pressure from thermal expansion or a tank that is overheating.

Never plug a dripping relief valve. We test it, check the incoming water pressure and look at whether a closed system has no place for expanding water to go. In that case, a thermal expansion tank is part of the repair, and we cover it in our water heater installation guidance.

Hard Water Scale And Sediment In Danvers Tanks

The Town's water report notes that white scaling or hard deposits on faucets and shower heads may come from calcium carbonate. Inside a tank, the same minerals settle as a crust on the bottom or on electric elements, which makes the heater louder, slower and less efficient. The Town does not publish a hardness number, so test your tap or ask the Water Division for its annual water quality report.

A yearly flush of a healthy tank removes loose sediment. If the drain valve is clogged solid or the tank is more than a decade old, flushing can start leaks, so we look before we recommend it.

When Repair Stops Making Sense

A cheap part on a young tank is a good repair. A part on a tank with a rusted base, a wet pan or an old failed anode is money spent on a heater that is about to leak. A leak from the tank body cannot be patched, and we say so on the first visit.

  • The tank itself is leaking, not a fitting
  • Rust-colored water comes only from the hot side
  • A repair costs a large share of a new unit
  • The heater is well past its usual service life
  • The same failure has happened more than once in a year

In those cases, we move to water heater replacement and walk you through sizing and fuel options. If you are considering a different type, our tankless water heater page explains the trade-offs.
