Water Heater Installation in Danvers, MA

A basement finish, a new addition or a switch away from oil can all call for water heater installation in Danvers, MA, and each one changes what the job involves. The fuel, the vent path, the location and the permit all need to be settled before the tank is set.

Installation means placing and connecting a new heater where there was none, or in a new spot or fuel. If you are simply swapping a failed tank, see our page on water heater replacement.

Planning Before The Tank Arrives

Most installation problems are planning problems. A heater has to sit on a level, protected floor, with room to service the burner or elements, a drain nearby and a path for its vent or power. Walls, closets and low basement ceilings in older Danvers houses often limit the choice.

About 43.5 percent of Danvers homes were built before 1960. Those houses often have a chimney flue sized for an older boiler, narrow stair access and a basement with a single floor drain. We look at all three before we suggest a unit, because the best heater on paper is useless if it will not fit through the bulkhead.

Fuel Type Conversions

Changing fuel is the largest variable in an installation. Going from electric to gas needs a gas line, a vent and a combustion air path. Going from gas to electric needs a dedicated circuit sized for the heater and capping of the old gas branch.

Gas service in Danvers is National Grid and electric service is the municipal Danvers Electric division, so a conversion may mean a call to the utility about meter capacity. We do not guess. We check the panel and the gas meter before quoting, so a surprise upgrade does not appear halfway through.

Venting In General Terms

A gas heater has to carry exhaust out of the house safely. That can be a metal flue into a lined chimney, a sidewall vent with a fan, or a sealed plastic vent for condensing units. The right one depends on the heater type and the available path, and the manufacturer lists what is allowed. A tankless water heater uses a different vent and a larger gas line than a tank.

Never reuse a corroded vent or tie a new high-efficiency heater to an old chimney without checking the liner. A loose or blocked flue pushes carbon monoxide into the home. Detectors on every floor are the safety net, but a correct vent is the real protection.

Permit And Licensing

The Massachusetts rule is that no plumbing work may be installed, altered, removed, replaced or repaired until a permit is issued. Permits go only to licensed plumbers, and only a master or journeyman licensed by the Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters may do the work. Danvers Inspectional Services at 1 Sylvan Street handles local permits. Customers in nearby Peabody deal with their own city office instead.

You can look up any plumber on the state's license lookup before you hire. We pull the permit as part of the job and schedule the inspection, so the work is on record.

Code Items That Come With Every Install

Under 248 CMR 10.14, a storage heater needs a properly sized temperature and pressure relief valve within 12 inches of the top of a vertical tank. Its discharge must be full size, with no shut-off valve, ending at a fixture or within 12 inches of the floor. A tank with a relief pipe aimed at the wall is a flood waiting to happen.

  • No check valve may be installed in the cold supply to the water heater
  • Residential hot water is set between 120 F minimum and 130 F maximum, with an exception noted
  • A thermal expansion tank is allowed by the code and required near the meter if a check valve or backflow preventer closes the system
  • A drain pan or floor drain protects finished space below or beside the tank

The expansion tank is the item homeowners miss most. Heating water expands, and if the plumbing is a closed loop, that pressure has nowhere to go but the relief valve or your fixtures.
