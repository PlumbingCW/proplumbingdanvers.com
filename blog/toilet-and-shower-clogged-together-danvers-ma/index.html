Toilet and Shower Clogged at the Same Time in Danvers, MA

One slow fixture is a clog. Two at once is a layout problem. When a toilet and a shower clog together in a Danvers, MA home, they almost always share a pipe, and the blockage sits downstream of where both of them connect.

The quickest way to learn how deep it is: watch what the other drains do. The steps below show you how.

Why Two Fixtures Fail Together

Inside the walls, each fixture has its own short drain that joins a larger branch line. Several branches then meet at the main drain, which runs to the sewer or septic tank. A blockage in a branch takes out the fixtures upstream of it, and a blockage in the main takes out every fixture lower than the clog.

The toilet and shower are often on the same bathroom branch, so a clog there is the most common cause. But when a washing machine or a basement floor drain also acts up, the main line is the suspect.

The Pattern Test

List what is slow

Walk the house. Note every sink, tub, washer and floor drain that gurgles, drains slowly or holds water.

Flush and watch

Flush the upstairs toilet and watch the lowest drain in the house. Bubbling or a rise in the basement floor drain means the main is blocked.

Run only the washer

If the shower or tub gurgles when the washing machine drains, the shared branch is carrying that water, and the clog is past both.

Check the cleanout

A capped cleanout fitting near the foundation or in the basement shows whether water sits in the main. Open it only with a bucket and gloves ready.

Do Not Do This

Mistakes that make it worse

  • ✓Do not keep flushing to see if it gets better, because every flush adds water to a stopped line.
  • ✓Do not run the washing machine or dishwasher.
  • ✓Do not pour chemical openers into a backed-up toilet, since they sit in the bowl and burn skin during repair.
  • ✓Do not push a small plunger against a main line problem, which only moves wastewater around.

Danvers Sewer Facts That Affect This Call

The Town of Danvers owns and repairs the sewer main line in the street, and its Sewer Division maintains over 125 miles of gravity main. Ask the Town where its responsibility ends and yours begins, and tell it if neighbors on your block have the same trouble. The Town also says flushable wipes do not break down, which makes them a frequent cause of shared-branch clogs.

Danvers requires a backwater valve where basement sewage discharge sits below the rim of the closest public sewer manhole, installed before the sewer connection leaves the building. If your basement fixtures are that low, a valve that sticks can cause a backup too.

Heavy rain matters here. Town reports rate flooding as high frequency in Danvers, with problem spots near Beaver Brook and the Crane River. Wet weather can stress the system, so tell your plumber if the backup began during a storm.

Branch Clog Or Main Line? Who Fixes It

A branch clog is a standard job for drain cleaning: a cable machine passes through the toilet flange or a cleanout, and a camera confirms the line is clear. That is the lower-cost route, and it usually restores both fixtures in one visit.

When the whole house is affected, the main needs real sewer line cleaning with a larger cutting head or jetting. Roots, bellies and broken clay can show up here, and a camera pass decides whether a repair follows.

What Happens On The Visit

The plumber confirms the pattern first, then picks an access point. For a bathroom branch that means pulling the toilet or using a cleanout, and for the main it means the cleanout closest to the street. A camera follows the cable to show roots, grease, scale or a sagging section.

Expect findings explained in plain terms and a price agreed before the work goes further. A bellied pipe or broken section is a repair question, handled separately from the cleaning itself.

What To Do While You Wait

Write down the time the first fixture slowed and what ran just before it. That timeline helps the plumber separate a one-time wad of paper from a slow, growing blockage.

Shut off water-using appliances, keep kids from flushing and move rugs and stored boxes away from floor drains. Open a window if sewer odor is strong. If water is coming up through a basement drain, keep power tools and electronics off the floor.

Neighboring Towns, Different Plumbing

In Peabody, city sewer pump stations on Russell Street and Northfield Road have been flagged in hazard reporting, so a backup during a storm may not be your pipe alone. In Middleton the first question is different, because septic is the norm there and a tank can be behind a backup that looks like a pipe problem.
