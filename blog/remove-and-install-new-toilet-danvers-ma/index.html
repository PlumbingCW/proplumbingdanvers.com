How to Remove and Install a New Toilet in Danvers, MA

Replacing a toilet takes about two hours, a helper and a good back. To remove and install a new toilet in Danvers, MA, you shut off the supply, drain the bowl, unbolt and lift the old one, check the flange, seat a new wax ring and set the new toilet straight.

The flange is where most DIY jobs go wrong, so we spend extra time on it.

Buy The Right Toilet Before You Start

Measure the rough-in, which is the distance from the finished wall to the center of the floor drain bolts. Twelve inches is the common figure, but ten and fourteen exist. A toilet built for twelve will not fit a ten-inch rough-in.

Check the bowl shape, round or elongated, and the height. Elongated bowls need about two inches more clearance in front. Choose a model with a quiet, fast-closing seat only if you like one, because the real decision is flush performance.

Tools And Parts Checklist

  • New toilet with tank bolts and a seat
  • Wax ring, or a wax-free seal, plus new closet bolts
  • Adjustable wrench, putty knife and a flat-head screwdriver
  • Braided stainless supply line, since the old one rarely survives
  • Sponge, bucket, rags and disposable gloves
  • Shims for a floor that is slightly out of level
  • Plumber's tape and a small level

Removing The Old Toilet

Shut off and drain

Close the stop valve behind the toilet, flush and hold the handle, then sponge the last water from tank and bowl.

Disconnect the supply

Loosen the supply line nut with a wrench and have a bucket handy for what drains out.

Unbolt and rock free

Remove the caps and nuts on the floor bolts, then rock the toilet gently to break the wax seal. Lift with a partner, because a toilet is heavier than it looks.

Scrape and cover

Scrape the old wax from the flange and stuff a rag in the pipe to block sewer gas while you work.

Check The Flange Before Anything Goes Back

The flange is the ring on the floor that the toilet bolts to. It should sit on top of the finished floor, level and solid, with no cracks. A flange recessed below the floor can leave the wax ring unable to seal, and that shows up as a slow leak and a rocking toilet.

A Quick Word On Wax Rings

A standard wax ring works for most floors. A thicker or flanged ring helps when the flange sits slightly low, while a wax-free seal can be reused if you must lift the toilet again. Whichever you pick, place it once and keep the toilet from shifting.

Setting The New Toilet

Install new bolts and the seal

Slide new closet bolts into the flange slots, then place the wax ring on the flange or on the horn of the new toilet.

Lower straight down

Align the bowl over the bolts and press it down evenly. Do not rock the toilet after it touches the wax.

Snug the nuts evenly

Tighten each side a little at a time. Overtightening cracks the porcelain, so stop at snug and level.

Attach the tank and supply

Bolt the tank to the bowl with the rubber gasket, connect the new supply line, open the stop valve and check every joint for drips.

When To Stop And Call

Call a plumber if you see these

  • ✓The flange is cracked, rusted through or loose from the floor.
  • ✓The floor around the toilet is soft.
  • ✓The stop valve will not close or leaks when turned.
  • ✓Water leaks at the base after you have seated the toilet and tightened the nuts.
  • ✓A new toilet clogs on the first day, which can point to a vent or drain problem.
