How to Repair a Leaky Shower Faucet in Danvers, MA

A shower that drips after you shut it off is almost always a worn cartridge, a tired stem washer or a pitted valve seat. To repair a leaky shower faucet in Danvers, MA, you shut off the water, pull the handle, swap the worn part and rebuild the trim, and most people finish in an hour or two.

This guide walks through that job in order, then marks the point where a plumber is the better call.

First, Work Out Which Drip You Have

Drip from the shower head

The valve is not sealing when closed. This points to the cartridge, the stem washer or the seat inside the wall.

Water around the handle

Water seeping behind the trim ring means a worn O-ring or a loose packing nut on the stem.

Drip from the tub spout

On a tub and shower combo, the diverter may be failing, or the same valve problem is leaking into the spout.

Hot or cold only

A drip only when the handle sits on hot suggests a worn hot-side seat or a damaged cartridge half.

Tools And Prep Before You Open The Wall Trim

Gather a Phillips and flat screwdriver, an adjustable wrench, needle-nose pliers, a hex key set, plumber's grease and a rag to stuff the drain so no screw goes down it. Take a photo of the handle before you remove it, because trim order is easy to forget.

Under the Massachusetts permit standard, fixing a leaky faucet is a minor repair, but replacing or relocating a faucet is not minor and needs a permit.

Find the fixture shutoff if the bathroom has one. If not, close the main shutoff and open a lower faucet to drain the pipes. Then turn the shower handle on to bleed off the last of the pressure.

The Cartridge Repair, Step By Step

Remove the handle

Pry off the cap, back out the set screw with the hex key and slide the handle and escutcheon plate free.

Pull the retaining clip or nut

Most cartridges are held by a brass clip or a bonnet nut. Take it out slowly and note which way the cartridge faces.

Extract the cartridge

Grip the stem with pliers and wiggle it straight out. If it fights you, a cartridge puller tool prevents cracked housings.

Install the match

Take the old cartridge to a plumbing supply counter. Grease the O-rings, seat the new one in the same orientation and rebuild the trim.

If You Have Old Compression Stems

Replace the washer

Unscrew the stem, remove the screw at its tip and swap the rubber washer for one of the same size and shape. A beveled washer will not seal in a flat-seat valve, so bring the old one with you.

Check the seat

Shine a flashlight into the valve body. A seat that is scored or pitted will chew through new washers within weeks, and a seat wrench can resurface or remove it. If the seat is stuck or the body is soldered into the wall, stop there.

Danvers Water And Your Shower Hardware

Danvers water comes mostly from Middleton Pond and is treated with pH adjustment and phosphates for corrosion control. The Town's own report notes that white scaling or hard deposits on faucets and shower heads may come from calcium carbonate. Scale grabs cartridge O-rings and wears them, so wipe mineral crust off the valve body before you reassemble.

The Town does not publish a hardness number. Ask the Water Division for the annual water quality report, or test your own tap, and use the result to decide whether a whole-house treatment step is worth a conversation.

State rules also matter. Massachusetts treats fixing a leaky faucet as minor work with no permit, but replacing or relocating the faucet is not minor and needs a permit issued to a licensed plumber.

When To Stop And Call A Plumber

Signs the job is bigger than a cartridge

  • ✓The handle will not come off or the cartridge will not budge after steady pulling.
  • ✓The valve seat is corroded and the body is soldered rather than threaded.
  • ✓Water stains show on the ceiling below or the wall feels soft around the tub.
  • ✓You replaced the cartridge and the drip is still there.
  • ✓The shutoff valves do not hold, so the water cannot be turned off.

A drip that keeps going after a good repair may mean the leak is inside the wall, not at the valve. Our faucet repair team handles the swap and the rebuild, and if water stains tell you the source is hidden, leak detection can trace it without tearing open tile.
