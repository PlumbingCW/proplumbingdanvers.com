Spring: When the Ipswich River Comes Up

In March 2010 record rainfall put the Ipswich River above flood stage for about 20 days and brought a state disaster declaration. The town's 2021 hazard plan also names flood-prone spots including Route 1, Salem Road, Rowley Bridge Road, the East Street culvert, Pond Street, Wildes Road, Bridge Road, Ipswich Road, River Road, Washington Street, Prospect Street and Perkins Road. If your home is near one of those roads, check your sump pit in March, not in April.

The Topsfield Fairgrounds on Boston Street can flood too. A wet spring that soaks a fairgrounds soaks neighboring basements, so listen for the sump pump cycling and see our sump pump page if it never stops. A pump that runs for days without catching up needs a look before it burns out.

According to Title 5 (310 CMR 15.301), a septic system must be inspected at the time of transfer of title, within two years before a sale, which matters if your Topsfield property is outside public sewer.

Summer: Drought Restrictions and Well-Pump Behavior

The 2016 drought warning ran from July to December and was the most severe since the 1980s, with the Ipswich River stopping in places. The town has issued mandatory outdoor water restrictions in July 2024 and June 2026, and those reach private well owners as well. In a dry spell a well can yield less, so low pressure, sputtering faucets or sandy sediment are worth a call before the pump runs dry and burns out.

The Topsfield Water Department serves about 80 percent of the town and the Masconomet Regional School District. Its two wells on North Street and Perkins Row sit in the Ipswich River Basin and are treated at a plant that has run since March 2019.

The wells carry high levels of iron and manganese, which can stain fixtures and shorten a water heater's life. Ask the water department for its report, and consider a water heater repair check if your tank is aging.

No official hardness figure is published, so test your tap before choosing any treatment. Homes outside the town water service should ask whether the property has its own well. Much of Topsfield is outside any public sewer, so also ask whether your property has a septic system.

Winter: Freeze Season and Burst Pipes

Pipes in exterior walls, crawl spaces and unheated garages are where freezes begin. If a faucet only trickles on a cold morning, the line may already be frozen. Open it, leave the water running slightly and keep cabinet doors open, and call us if you hear or see water where it should not be, since a split pipe may already be waiting to thaw. Our burst pipe repair crews can respond around the clock.

Before a long winter trip, shut the main, drain the lines and keep the heat on. In a well house, also protect the pressure tank and pump lines from the cold. A frozen well pump is a call to make in daylight if you can, but never wait on a burst.

Before We Arrive: Well, Pump and Freeze Checks

Check pressure and the pump breaker

If the water has gone weak or stopped, look at the pressure gauge and the pump breaker. Reset a tripped breaker once, then stop and call if it trips again.

Find where the water enters

Locate the main shutoff, which may be after the pressure tank in a well house. For a burst, close it fully and open a low faucet to drain the lines.

Thaw with care

Use a hair dryer on a frozen pipe, never a torch or open flame. Keep the faucet open while you thaw, and stop if you find a split.

Photograph staining and sediment

If water looks orange, brown or sandy, collect a clear jar and photograph the fixture stains. It helps us decide whether the cause is the well, the tank or the pipe.
