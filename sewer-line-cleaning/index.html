Warning Signs Before the Basement Floods

Main lines rarely fail without hints. A toilet that gurgles when the washer drains, a floor drain that bubbles, slow drains on every level and a faint sewer smell all say the main is partly blocked. Acting at the gurgle stage is far cheaper than cleaning a flooded basement.

According to the Town, sewage from Danvers goes to the South Essex Sewerage District and is treated at its Salem plant, which is the standard destination for every clean-out downstream of your house.

Cleanouts: The Door to Your Main Line

A cleanout is a capped access pipe on the sewer line, usually near the foundation or in the basement. It lets us run a cable or jetting hose directly into the main without removing a toilet. A homeowner who knows where it is saves time in an emergency.

Not every house has one. If there is none, we pull a toilet or open a stack, and we may recommend installing a cleanout for future service. That small addition makes every future cleaning faster and cheaper.

Roots, Wipes and Grease: What Actually Clogs a Main

Roots enter through joints and form a mat. Grease cools and sticks to pipe walls. Wipes labeled flushable are a third cause, and the Town of Danvers says they do not break down and they clog sewer pipes. Paper towels, feminine products and dental floss behave the same way.

A cleaning removes today's clog, not the cause. If roots are the reason, expect them back unless the joint is fixed. If wipes are the cause, the cleaning may last for years once they stay out of the toilet.

A Maintenance Rhythm That Fits Your Line

Some lines never need regular service, and others do. A house with big mature trees near the lateral and older clay or cast iron pipe is a candidate for a periodic cleaning, perhaps every year or two. A newer plastic line with a clean camera record can go much longer.

The best schedule comes from a camera pass after a cleaning, which shows how fast buildup returns. See sewer inspection for what the camera reveals, then decide on a rhythm that makes sense.

Who Owns the Blockage: Town Main or Your Pipe

The Town of Danvers owns and repairs the main line sewer, which runs through streets and easements. Your lateral runs from the house toward that main. If a neighbor's drain is also backing up, the clog may sit in the town main, which is a call to the Sewer Division.

We can help you tell the difference. Cleaning from your cleanout and watching the flow, then running a camera, shows whether the blockage is on your side. If it is not, we say so and you avoid paying for work that is not yours.

Sewage from Danvers travels to the South Essex Sewerage District and its Salem plant. During heavy storms the system can fill, and basements below the nearest manhole rim are at risk, which is why Danvers requires a backwater valve in that situation.
