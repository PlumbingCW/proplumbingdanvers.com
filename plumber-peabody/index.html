Drain Backups and Sewer Capacity in Peabody

Peabody is a co-permittee in the South Essex Sewerage District alongside Beverly, Danvers, Marblehead and Salem, and the city posts sanitary sewer overflow notices on its website. If a notice lines up with the day your lowest drain gurgled, the cause may be shared capacity and not your own pipe. Check the notices first, then call so we can tell the two apart.

When the notices are clear, the blockage is almost always on your side of the property line: roots in the lateral, grease in a kitchen line, or a sagging section that holds water. A camera pass shows which. Our drain cleaning work starts with a cable or jetting pass, and we only bring up sewer line repair when the camera finds a cracked or collapsed section.

One clue saves time. If only the basement tub or floor drain backs up while the upstairs sinks run fine, the clog sits downstream of where those fixtures join the main. If every fixture is slow, the clog is closer to the house, and running more water only pushes the problem into the lowest opening.

Brook Flooding, Wet Basements and Sump Pumps

Peabody's state hazard-planning report says Proctor Brook, Goldthwaite Brook and Strongwater Brook frequently flood during moderate and severe rain, and that downtown floods too. Homes near those brooks sit on ground that fills fast, so a sump pump there works hardest when the power is most likely to flicker. Pour a bucket of water into the pit before the forecast turns bad and watch that the float lifts and the pump discharges.

March 2018 brought three winter storms with rain flooding, coastal surge and power outages that ran for days. A pump wired only to house current is useless in that situation, so ask about a battery backup if your basement has ever taken water. If water keeps coming back even with a healthy pump, the trouble is probably outside the foundation, and basement waterproofing is the longer fix to discuss.

The same report flagged the city's sewer pump stations on Russell Street and Northfield Road. A sewage smell or a backup after a heavy storm is worth reporting to the city as well as to us, because it may not be a problem inside your walls at all.

What To Do In Your First Ten Minutes in a Wet or Backed-Up House

Check the pump, then the breaker

Look at the sump pit. If the pump is silent and the pit is full, unplug it and check the outlet and the breaker. Never wade through standing water to reach a panel, and call us if the floor around it is wet.

Find the main shutoff

The main usually sits where the water line enters the basement, often near the meter. If a pipe has burst, close it fully. If water is entering from outside, shutting it off will not help, but you will know where it is.

Stay off the lowest drains

Sewage that cannot leave the house comes up the lowest opening. Skip showers, laundry and dishwashers until we have looked, and keep children and pets out of the area.

Lift what water ruins

Get boxes, rugs and electronics off the floor and take photos of the water line for your records. Do not run a wet-dry vacuum near standing water if the outlet is also wet.

Water Supply and Lead Service Lines

Peabody's tap water comes mainly from the city's reservoir system, with the Winona Pond, Suntaug Lake and Spring Pond sources, and the city adds supplemental MWRA water in high-demand months. The city has not published a hardness or pH figure we can quote. If the taste or clarity of your water shifts in summer, ask the Peabody Water Department for its annual water quality report or test your tap.

The city runs a Lead Service Line Replacement Program and removed 48 lead goosenecks according to its 2022 report. Its lead 90th percentile reading in August 2022 was 2.64 ppb against an action level of 15 ppb. That number describes the system, not your pipe, so ask the water department what material your own service line is. If it needs replacing, our water service line repair page explains the process.
