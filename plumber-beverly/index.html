Coastal Ground and Low Streets Set the Rules

Beverly's own hazard report names a long list of flood-prone spots: Elliott Street and Route 62 near the McKay Street culvert, Federal Street, Marsh Avenue, Fosters Point, Willow Street, Quincy Park, and Route 127 at the West Street and Hale Street rail crossing. If your home sits on or near one of them, treat any heavy rain as a basement event and know where your main shutoff and sump pit are before the storm arrives.

The Mother's Day storm of 2006 dropped more than 12 inches of rain and caused severe flooding across the city. Storms of that size overload storm drains and soak the ground until water finds the foundation. After a rain like that, a basement that was dry for years can suddenly need a sump pump or at least a close look at the one it has.

The Water From Your Tap: Salem and Beverly Water Supply Board

Beverly's water comes from the Salem and Beverly Water Supply Board, which draws on the Ipswich River and three reservoirs: Wenham Lake, Putnamville Reservoir and Longham Reservoir, according to its 2024 water quality report. The board adjusts pH for corrosion control, which is aimed at protecting the pipes between the street and your faucet. If you see blue-green staining at old copper fittings, tell us, because it may point to a local pipe issue.

The same report says the board completed a service-line inventory and identified no lead exceedances. That is good news for the system and still does not describe your own pipe. No hardness figure is published, so test your tap or ask the supply board before you size a softener or a tankless unit, since scale affects a water heater more than almost anything else.

Floor Drains, Backflow and Storm Surge

Beverly shares the South Essex Sewerage District permit with Danvers and Peabody, and the state hazard report lists sewer pump stations on Marsh Street, Beach Street and Margin Street as highly susceptible to coastal flooding. When a station is overwhelmed, wastewater can back toward the lowest drain in a house. That drain is usually the basement floor drain, so keep it clear and watch it during storms.

A backwater valve on the building drain can stop reverse flow, but it only works if it is properly installed and kept clean. We can look at your lateral and floor drain together, and a drain cleaning visit often reveals whether the problem is a local blockage or flow coming back from the street.

Salt Air, Sea Walls and Outdoor Fittings

The 2017 coastal storms damaged sea walls at Lynch Park and Endicott College and caused erosion at Obear Park and Independence Park. That is a reminder of how much salt-laden weather the shoreline takes. Homes in places like Fosters Point and Beverly Cove should expect faster corrosion on hose bibs, outdoor shutoffs and exposed fittings, so inspect them each spring and replace any that look pitted or weep.

While You Wait: Floor Drains First, Then the Rest

Watch the floor drain

If water is rising from a basement floor drain, stop all water use and keep everyone away from the area. Do not pour chemical cleaners into a drain that is already backing up.

Cut power if water is near outlets

Turn off the breaker for the basement only if you can reach the panel without stepping in water. Otherwise stay out and call us, and tell the dispatcher the water is near electrical equipment.

Shut off the main for a burst

A burst supply pipe needs the main closed fast. Know where it is, turn it fully, then open a low faucet to drain what remains in the lines.

Photograph and move valuables

Take pictures of the water line and lift stored items off the floor. After a coastal storm, note whether the water smells of salt or sewage, because we ask.
